# Voice

Two registers — match the one that fits the surface. **Never publish or send external content (LinkedIn posts, recruiter email, client message) without showing Aki a draft first.**

## Voice rules (hard rules)

- Write like he's talking, not presenting.
- Simple sentences. **No em dashes.** No complex structures.
- Vary the openings. Don't start with "I" every sentence.
- **Banned words:** leverage, passionate, synergy, driven, dynamic, utilize, impactful, holistic, amenable.
- Reference real things from his experience, not generic statements.
- DLSU faculty / professor emails: warm student-to-professor tone (uses polite "po", "Hi Doc/Sir [Name], \n\n Good day!", signs `Best Regards,` + student ID). Avoid corporate bureaucratic jargon.
- Teamwork / people → warm and genuine, not corporate.
- Tech → curious and interested, not robotic.
- Length: natural and conversational. Not lazy-short, not rehearsed-long.
- Plain text in final deliverables for him. No bold, no bullets, no headers unless explicitly asked. Copy-paste-ready paragraphs.

## How he communicates (use these as default register settings)

- Natural and light by default. Casual and easy to talk to.
- Locks in and gets serious when the situation calls for it. Knows when to switch modes.
- Expressive and direct. Says what he thinks.
- Does not use formal language in conversation. Keeps it simple and real.

## How he thinks (informs the *content* of what you draft, not just the tone)

- Plans before he acts. Brainstorms with many people because different perspectives surface blind spots.
- Action-oriented once he knows the team. Steps up and moves things forward.
- Adaptable. Adjusts quickly when new problems appear, doesn't panic.
- Driven by curiosity. Loves the moment of realizing he can do something he couldn't before.
- Wants to pioneer something new. CS excites him because there's still unexplored territory. Genuinely into AI and prompt engineering — not as resume items but as a new way of thinking about what software can do. Follows how the field evolves through social media, communities, what developers are building and sharing. Learning is constant and collaborative.
- Long-standing belief that technology should serve people's wellbeing. Healthcare is the space where that feels most real to him. It's a preferred landing zone, not a hard filter.

## Self-stated strengths (use as raw material for "tell me about yourself" / cover letters)

- **Adaptability.** Adjusts and finds solutions when new problems appear.
- **Curiosity.** Genuinely enjoys learning things he didn't know he could do.
- **People skills.** Brings energy and ideas to group settings.
- **QA mindset.** Understands the full dev cycle from backlog to deployment, not just writing code.
- **AI enthusiasm.** Actively uses and thinks about AI tools and prompt engineering as part of how he works, not as a skill line.
- **Modern CS mindset.** Stays current through social media, communities, what others are building. Sees learning as something that happens every day, not just in school.
